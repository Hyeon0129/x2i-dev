## Part 1: Wait, I'm a Father?

I was born in November 1998. Grew up in an ordinary household, went to middle school and high school like everyone else. In July 2017, I enlisted in the military at 20 years old  18 by international standards. Two years later, April 2019, I was discharged. Back in the world with zero plan.

Why Japan? Honestly, looking back now, I can piece it together. I was young and had somehow convinced myself I could do anything. Japanese was supposed to be easy to pick up for Korean speakers. And there were all these articles and social media posts floating around saying IT jobs in Japan were wide open for foreigners. Probably a mix of all that. After getting discharged, I did manual labor for a month, stacked that money on top of what I'd saved in the military, and left for Japan with about $2,200 total.

Everything was foreign from day one. I couldn't make out a single word. Taking the subway was genuinely confusing. At a convenience store, when the cashier asked if I needed a bag, I had no idea what she was saying and just responded in English. "No, it's okay." What the hell was I thinking, going to another country without studying the language even a little? I genuinely still don't know.

A month or two into it, on some ordinary summer day in July 2019, I got a message from her. We had met once before and had been staying in touch since. But that day something felt off. She kept dancing around something, never quite getting to the point. The longer the conversation went on, the more one thought kept creeping in: *no way... it can't be... she's not going to tell me what I think she's about to tell me.* Nothing was confirmed that day. She said she'd show me a test tomorrow. I didn't sleep a single second that night.

At 4:34 AM, a photo came through. With trembling fingers, I opened it.
 
***Two lines.***

There's a thing that happens when your mind gets hit with something it genuinely cannot process. I once had a 500kg pallet come down directly on my hand. For about five seconds I couldn't make a single sound. That's exactly what this was. I opened that photo and went completely silent. Not just quiet. Silent.

No thoughts came. I just went outside and sat on the bench in front of my place and stayed there for the rest of the day. Cigarette after cigarette. I didn't even know what I was supposed to be thinking about. I just smoked.

After a long time, I finally replied. ***"I'll take responsibility."***  Whether it was instinct or just something I blurted out in the moment, I couldn't say back then. Now I think it was instinct. I told her, plainly: *I'll take responsibility.* My mother found out five months later. I told no one  not my parents, not a single friend. I already knew what they'd say. There was no point in hearing it.

I remembered watching a TV show years before, when I was still in high school. There was a young couple who'd had a kid right after turning 20 and were clearly struggling to get by. I remember watching them and thinking, *wow, having a kid that young must be rough.* That was the extent of how much I'd ever thought about it.
 
And now it was me.

But none of that changed anything. I didn't want an abortion. A life that carries my DNA exists somewhere in this world. How do you turn your back on that? And if I ran, I already knew I'd carry it for the rest of my life. In a strange way, running would've been the harder choice.

So there I was. In Japan, suddenly a father-in-waiting, with no Japanese, no job, and money that was quietly disappearing. No clear answer came no matter how long I sat with it. Somewhere far away, that tiny life was growing, slowly and steadily.

One thing was certain: I had to find a part-time job.
